Multi-task ranking is a structure in which the ranker does not predict "will this user like this item" as a single scalar. It predicts a separate probability for each kind of action, then combines them into one score and sorts by it. An output that produces one probability per action is called a head.

score = Σ_k  w_k · P(action_k | user, item)

However, this weighted sum is only one of several ways to combine them. Within the same company, Meta published a additive form, a multiplicative form, and a multiplicative penalty as formulas for different surfaces, each in a different post (table in the Meta case). "Multi-task ranking = weighted sum" is an assumption, not an observation.

There are four design decisions that actually have to be made here.

  • Which actions to use as heads
  • Which form to combine them in
  • How to mix in negative actions
  • Where to get the weights w_k

⚠ Every claim in these notes that is based on the Meta system card (a regulatory-response document in which the Transparency Center publishes the list of prediction heads per surface) comes from a snapshot read on 2026-08-09. The number of prediction heads is a typical example. The full caveats are collected in the "system card citation shelf life" section of ../services/facebook.md.

Why this structure

Splitting heads per action and keeping separate weights lets you change the objective without a code deployment and also protects the training signal of sparse actions inferred. The cost is that the weights become product policy directly.

  • The objective can change without a code deployment. The model outputs only probabilities, and the product judgment (how many times more a retweet matters than a like) is separated into the weights inferred. X separating all weights into runtime injection is consistent with this reading.
  • Putting sparse actions (report, block) in separate heads keeps their training signal from vanishing. Merging them into one scalar lets common actions drown them out inferred.
  • There is also a cost. The weights become product policy directly, and even at X those values were not in the public source until the Gen3 release. The X case shows this problem best.

Case: Meta — the combination form differs by surface

Meta published 4 combination forms within one company (counting the additive form with negative terms separately from the plain additive form). The combination form Facebook published is linear only, while Instagram published that it uses three different forms for different problems.

Surface Form Formula (verbatim unless noted) Year
Facebook Feed Additive Vijt = wijt1·Yijt1 + wijt2·Yijt2 + … + wijtk·Yijtk 2021
Instagram Suggested Posts Multiplicative Value(Post) = (probability_like)^weight_like * (1- probability_not_interested)^weight_see_less 2020
Instagram Explore Additive (with negative term) Expected Value = W_click * P(click) + W_like * P(like) – W_see_less * P(see less) + etc. 2023
Instagram Notifications Multiplicative penalty Score(c) = R(c) × D(c), D(c) = ∏_{i=1..m} (1 - w_i · p_i(c)) ⚠ not verbatim (explained below the table) 2025

The formula in the Notifications row is not verbatim. The original is a LaTeX image, and the two formulas in the table are a transcription of that image observed 2026-08-09. The multiplicative structure and the definitions of R and D are supported by the body prose verified — feed-diversity.md.

  • The source of the Facebook Feed formula is the 2021 post verified — How machine learning powers Facebook's News Feed ranking algorithm.
  • The source of the Instagram Suggested Posts formula is the 2020 post verified — Designing a Constrained Exploration System.
  • The source of the Instagram Explore formula is the 2023 post verified — Scaling the Instagram Explore recommendations system.
  • The source of the Instagram Notifications formula is the 2025 post verified — A New Ranking Framework for Better Notification Quality on Instagram.

Context for each surface is in facebook.md and instagram.md. Only the form comparison is made here.

How to read this

Multiplicative and additive forms give negative signals different authority inferred. However, the 2020 multiplicative form and the 2023 additive form are on different surfaces, and there is no source comparing before and after on the same surface inferred.

  • The weight w in the Facebook formula is indexed by (i,j,t). This means the weights are personalized per post, user, and point in time verified 2021.
    • The original text says: "for each post i, we estimate Yijt", "the characteristics of the post X_it toward viewer j at time t". So i is the post and j is the viewer.
    • However, since only the symbolic formula is available, we cannot confirm whether the actual implementation really depends on i (the post) inferred.
  • Multiplicative and additive forms give negative signals different authority inferred. This judgment follows directly from the form of the formula.
    • In the multiplicative form, if P(not_interested) → 1 the value collapses to 0, so it is effectively a hard veto inferred.
    • In the additive form, no matter how large the negative term is, other terms can outweigh it, so it is an exchangeable cost inferred.
  • Taking the log of the multiplicative form also gives a linear expression Σ w·log P inferred. That is, it is the same as Facebook's Σ w·P in being a "linear combination", and the only difference is whether P or log P is used.
  • ⚠ The 2020 multiplicative form and the 2023 additive form must not be read as a "transition". The surfaces differ (Suggested Posts and Explore). There is no source comparing before and after on the same surface inferred.
  • The fourth, the Notifications formula, is a diversity penalty rather than a combination. Details are in feed-diversity.md.

The opposite of X — whether negatives are multiplied

On the question of whether to multiply negative probabilities into the score, the two companies went in opposite directions.

  • Instagram 2020 multiplies in the negative probability to give a veto ((1 - P_not_interested)^w) verified 2020.
  • X 2025 deliberately does not multiply. It only adds a constant penalty when a threshold is exceeded verified (design decision 2).
  • X 2026 multiplies, but compresses a negative total score to a 0.001 scale so that it never mixes with positive candidates verified — params/config.rs:40 (Gen4 47c1bcd).

X spent two generations keeping negative heads from changing the ranking much, while Instagram instead uses the property that the score collapses to 0 in the multiplicative form as a feature inferred. Neither the code nor the blog posts state why the two companies designed it this way.

Case: X — 6 design decisions

In X's public code, the head list and the way negative heads are mixed in differ by generation verified (design decision 2, design decision 3). In 2026 Rust, even after the weighted sum, the author-diversity decay and the OON (out-of-network, candidates from accounts the user does not follow) multiplier change the score, and VMRanker can overwrite that score verified (design decision 6). The 2026 Rust descriptions and line numbers in this section are as of the Gen3 release (2026-05-15), and the 24 heads in the Gen4 README and the deletion of the ranking scorer in the 2026-09-24 commit (Gen4+28) are in the x.md ranking section verified.

2. Whether negative heads go into the multiplication

Whether to put negative heads into the multiplication is where the generations diverge most. X 2025 does not multiply and only adds a constant penalty when a threshold is exceeded verified. 2026 Rust changed the approach and multiplies negative heads in as they are. Instead, if the total score becomes negative, it compresses that whole score verified.

The X 2025 aggregation code multiplies positive weights into the score and adds them, and for negative weights it adds only the weight value when a threshold is exceeded verified — home-mixer/.../util/RerankerUtil.scala:106-121.

The reason for not multiplying is not written in the code, so we leave it as an inference. Negative heads have small probabilities and large noise, so multiplying them in makes the score swing widely inferred. Looking only at whether the threshold was exceeded makes it a step function, which is stable inferred.

It matters that the normalization reference is a batch-relative value within the request. Even for the same post, whether the negative penalty fires changes if the composition of the candidates scored together changes verified — RerankerUtil.scala:43-65.

  • The normalization formula is score / maxHeadScore, and the denominator is the per-head maximum over all candidates in that request.

There are also signs of a transition. When EnableNegSectionRankingParam is turned on, the formula changes to combinedScore + weight * (1.0 min (score + 0.1)). Just above it is a comment telling people to ship and clean this up as soon as possible ("This should be shipped and cleaned as soon as possible") verified — RerankerUtil.scala:115-118.

Note that in the repo default state, negative heads produce no deduction at all. All 4 related booleans are false verified — HomeGlobalParams.scala:545-573.

  • The four booleans are NormalizedNegativeHead, ConstantNegativeHead, UseWeightForNegHeadParam, and EnableNegSectionRankingParam.

The function that compresses a negative total score in 2026 Rust is below verified — ranking_scorer.rs:175-183 (Gen3 0bfc279).

This function is a direct port of the 2025 Scala function. The three-way branch corresponds line by line, in the same order verified — RerankerUtil.scala:130-135.

The variables correspond as follows.

  • modelWeightsSum ↔ w.total_sum
  • negativeModelWeightsSum ↔ w.negative_sum
  • Epsilon ↔ NEGATIVE_SCORES_OFFSET

The constant values are also the same in the two generations. The corresponding 2025 constant is val Epsilon = 0.001 (RerankerUtil.scala:16), and NEGATIVE_SCORES_OFFSET, which was not public in Gen3, is confirmed as 0.001 in the Gen4 (47c1bcd) release verified — params/config.rs:40. The two repos are known to have "0 code duplication", but the score aggregation formula was ported verified.

In this formula, a negative total score is squashed to a 0.001 scale. Since the minimum of a positive candidate is 0 + 0.001 = 0.001, a negative candidate always ends up below every positive candidate. The design preserves order while keeping the two groups from mixing.

The same formula also remains in the file weighted_scorer.rs:87-89, which is no longer used. This file reads p::NEGATIVE_WEIGHTS_SUM / p::WEIGHTS_SUM as constants. In other words, it is likely the form from the generation before the structure changed to compute the weight sum per request inferred.

6. Scores keep changing after the weighted sum

The weighted sum is not the end. As of the Gen3 release (2026-05-15), in 2026 Rust an offset, normalization, author-diversity decay, and an OON multiplier are applied in turn after the weighted sum, and then VMRanker can overwrite all scores verified.

  1. After computing the per-head weighted sum, apply offset_score (ranking_scorer.rs:146-183).
  2. Apply normalize_score(candidate, raw). In Gen3 the function body was not public (the crate::util module was missing). The ranking scorer in Gen4 (47c1bcd) does not call this function, and home-mixer/util/ is public verified — ranking_scorer.rs.
  3. Author-diversity decay: multiply the nth post of the same author by (1 - floor) · decay^n + floor (:186-217).
  4. OON multiplier: multiply only candidates that are not in-network by effective_oon (:220-239, 272-275).
  5. Then VMRanker, which optimizes the slate with a DPP (determinantal point process), can overwrite all scores (vm_ranker.rs:47-52).

We confirmed this order in ranking_scorer.rs:248-284 and phoenix_candidate_pipeline.rs:300 of Gen3 0bfc279 verified.

2025 Scala has a stage of the same nature, but its approach is additive. MMR (maximal marginal relevance) re-ranking adds the minimum distance to already selected candidates, weighted, to the relevance score, and overwrites ScoreFeature with that value verified — DiversityRescoringFeatureHydrator.scala:70,102.

  • The formula is score = relevance + diversityWeight · minDistance, and DiversityRescoringFeatureHydrator performs this computation.

The diversity layer is covered in detail in feed-diversity.md.

Case: Netflix — leaves the head × weight frame itself

Netflix does not fit the X/Meta-style frame of multiplying per-head probabilities by weights and summing. Its published objective is a reward function, not a weighted sum. The X/Meta frame written as a formula is Σ w·P(action).

  • It uses a contextual bandit frame. The recommendation is the action, and the user response is the reward verified — Recommending for Long-Term Member Satisfaction at Netflix, 2024.
  • The north star (final goal metric) is retention, but it is not optimized directly. It is a sparse, noisy signal at the level of "1 time per account per month", so it cannot be attributed to individual recommendations verified (same post).
  • The published proxy reward has the form r(user, item) = f(play, complete, thumb). It is a function of observed actions, not a weighted sum of per-action probability predictions. The decomposition into a prediction head list and a weight vector does not appear at all verified (same post).
  • It supplements delayed feedback with prediction. A Delayed Feedback Prediction Model predicts p(final feedback | observed feedback) and feeds it into the reward verified (same post).
    • Thumbs can arrive several days after a view, which conflicts with real-time training, and this model addresses that problem head-on verified (same post).
    • The X and Meta literature has no corresponding mechanism.
  • It states the problem of CTR over-optimization explicitly: "CTR over-optimization encourages clickbait" verified (same post).
  • It approaches the problem as a reward engineering loop, not weight tuning verified (same post). Viewed as a "method" of setting weights, it is the third answer after Facebook (survey correlation) and Instagram (Bayesian opt).
    • The loop's order is hypothesis → reward revision → bandit policy training → A/B verified (same post).

However, a different frame does not mean there are no heads. FM (Netflix's personalization recommendation foundation model) has multi-token and multiple prediction heads verified — FM blog 2025. It also uses a weighted objective ("a 5-minute trailer and a 2-hour finish cannot be the same") verified (same post). The difference is that the weighting lives inside the training objective, and a serving-time Σ w·P combination layer is absent from the public literature. In GenRec (2026), an LLM trained with a reward-weighted loss ranks directly verified — GenRec blog. The combination is absorbed into the model parameters.

The % Match shown on screen is for display, not a ranking score. This value is computed "from viewing habits and behavior analysis alone" verified — 2017 talk. It is a case that explicitly shows a system where the number shown to users differs from the number used for sorting (netflix.md).

What generalizes from this case

Putting several services side by side, the combination form differs by surface, the number of heads by source, and the way negative signals are handled by company.

  • "Weighted sum" is an assumption, not an observation. Meta alone has published 4 kinds by surface: addition, multiplication, addition with negative terms, and a multiplicative penalty. When looking at a new service, do not start by taking Σ w·P as the default assumption.
  • Even when "what the model predicts" is published, "what it treats as important" is not. Only the items disclosed differ; what is hidden is the same.
    • X has the head list in code and the weights in configuration. Through Gen3 those values were not in the repo, and since the Gen4 release the defaults are mirrored in the repo verified.
    • Meta has the head list on the card, the method of setting weights in blog posts, and the values nowhere.
  • The direction in which negative signals are handled is opposite across companies. X avoids multiplication (2025 constant penalty, 2026 negative total score compression), while Instagram 2020 multiplies to give a veto. Opposite designs for the same problem are both in production.
  • The number of heads depends on the kind of source. Source code disagrees by file, and system cards by document. To compare numbers, first ask whether the two numbers are of the same kind.
    • X differs by file (19/21/22/15/13/17).
    • Meta differs by document (10 on the card and negative predictions only in the body text).
  • The weighted-sum score is not the final ranking. Diversity, OON, and slate optimization follow.
    • In fact, X 2026 stores both weighted_score and score as separate fields verified — ranking_scorer.rs:277-281 (Gen3 0bfc279).
    • Meta also has a separate context pass 2 after the pointwise (scoring each candidate separately) pass 1 verified 2021.
  • The Σ w·P frame may be a practice of ads-style surfaces, not universal. Netflix solves the same problem (unifying multiple behavior signals) with a reward function and bandits. When the business model is subscription (retention), the objective also leans toward a long-term satisfaction proxy rather than maximizing session engagement. The choice of frame may be a function of the revenue model, not of technology inferred.
  • The reason the north star is not optimized directly is signal density. True goals such as retention that arrives 1 time a month or sparse surveys are pushed to the proxy reward and weight-decision layers, and the ranker handles dense behavior signals.
    • Facebook's survey correlation and Netflix's reward engineering are two implementations of the same structure.

Who uses this

X, Facebook, and Instagram use this structure verified. Threads published 10 heads but has no information on the combination method or weights, and Netflix uses a reward function and bandits instead of a weighted sum verified.

  • X: All three generations, 2023, 2025, and 2026, use this structure. The number of heads differs by generation and file (Gen3 19–22, Gen4 README 24, Design decision 3). Weights are injected through FeatureSwitch and query feature verified — x.md.
  • Facebook: It uses the additive form Vijt = Σ wijtk·Yijtk. Pass 1 is a pointwise multi-task neural network, and weights are set by survey correlation. The Feed card has 10 heads, and there are more negative predictions only in the body text verified — facebook.md.
  • Instagram: It has 3 combination forms (2020 multiplicative, 2023 additive with negative terms, 2025 multiplicative penalty). Weights are set by offline replay/offline tuning and Bayesian optimization. It has a separate MTML model per surface × component (ig_stories_tray_mtml, more than 1,000 in production) verified — instagram.md.
  • Threads: It published 10 heads, but has 0 information on the combination method and weights, and 0 negative heads. This is the clearest evidence that the disclosure level differs by surface even within the same company verified — threads.md.
  • Netflix: Not of the Σ w·P form. It combines a proxy reward r=f(play, complete, thumb) and delayed feedback prediction in a bandit. The north star is retention verified — netflix.md.
  • Other services: Not yet investigated. YouTube's multi-gate MoE (mixture-of-experts, 2019 RecSys) and the Pinterest family are likely in the same category, but this repository has not yet confirmed them with a primary source. This is an open question.
