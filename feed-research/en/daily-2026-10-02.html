1VF now decides the blocked actions and notice text for action limits — X

Source xai-org/x-algorithm@b79b947 — public source code, commit of 2026-10-01 02:13 UTC. These notes number X commits after the Gen4 release in order, and this commit is Gen4+33. The total change is +2,490/−506 across 47 files. Of these, 4 are new files: 3 new in VF (829 lines, of which the two limited-actions files account for 703 lines) and 1 new in abuse-enforcement (173 lines). phoenix-rankall config/mod.rs is modified by +84 lines.

Background visibility-filtering (VF) is a service that decides, for each post, whether it may be shown to this viewer. X's For You timeline layers two kinds of visibility decisions onto ranked candidates. One is the Withheld axis of the Verdict tree, which issues drops and tombstones; the other is the restriction axis, which additionally applies a media interstitial or limited engagement to a Shown post. LimitedEngagement on the second axis means the viewer cannot perform actions such as reply, like, or retweet on that tweet. Until now, the code carried only "one reason"; which of the 26 actions to block and what to show in the UI was mapped by downstream home-mixer (the service that assembles the For You feed) or by the client. This commit brings that whole mapping into VF, declares the policy as data (FeatureSwitches and a stringcenter bundle), and promotes the payload sent over the wire into a list of "action × localized Prompt".

What we learnedA. VF sets the actions to block and the notice text for each restriction reason as data

VF has a new limited-actions policy system, made up of two new files totaling 703 lines. There are five restriction reasons: ConversationControl, ReadonlyViewer, BlockedViewer, RootAuthorBlockedViewer, and StaleTweet. In code, these five are variants of LimitedEngagementReason.

  • The new policy file takes a restriction reason and resolves, through FeatureSwitches, the list of actions to block for that reason and the combination of copy settings verified — limited_actions_policy.rs:14-115.

    • The file is VF visibility-filtering/params/limited_actions_policy.rs, with 306 new lines.
    • The input is LimitedEngagementReason. What it resolves is the LimitedActionType list attached to that reason and the combination of copy namespace / prompt_type / learn_more_url.
    • The return value is LimitedActionsPolicies (reason → Vec<LimitedAction>).

  • The restriction information changed from a structure that wrapped a single reason to one that accumulates multiple reasons verified.

What we learnedE. abuse-enforcement-service applies Kafka config changes by restarting

abuse-enforcement-service is the service that decides actual actions (label/challenge/suspension), and its Rust rewrite was released on 2026-09-23. Starting with this commit, the service captures its boot-time config (boot_config) and builds the Kafka producers on top of it. The captured value is a serde_json::Value. Previously, lib.rs pulled the producers out with DynamicConfig.kafka_producers(). A dedicated module (restart_on_config_change.rs) that restarts the service when the Kafka config changes in operation was also added. As a result, the kafka producer binding has narrowed to react only to the "boot-time snapshot".

  • The new module compares the config captured at boot with the live config in operation, and prompts a restart of the service if a designated path changes verified — restart_on_config_change.rs:1-85.
    • restart_on_config_change.rs in abuse-enforcement-service is new, at 173 lines.
    • The value captured at boot is a serde_json::Value, and the watched targets are designated JSON pointer paths.
    • There are three default watched paths: /kafka/consumer/topic_labels, /topic_labels, and /kafka/producer.
    • The result has three states (Check::{Unchanged, Invalid, Restart(Vec<String>)}). If nothing changed, it is Unchanged.
    • If something changed, it runs validate(live). If validation passes, the result is Restart (the list of changed paths); if it fails, the result is Invalid. On Invalid, it does not restart and only logs.
  • A metric signaling a pending restart is reported with two labels, valid and invalid verified.
    • The metric name is CONFIG_RESTART_PENDING.
  • The function that builds the Kafka producers was changed to take the boot-time config snapshot instead of the live config verified — lib.rs:831-840, 1385-1392.
    • In lib.rs (+325/−55), the signature changed from build_kafka_producers(cfg, dynamic_config) to build_kafka_producers(cfg, boot_config).
    • boot_config is the boot-time config snapshot captured with dynamic_config.config().unwrap_or(serde_json::Value::Null). Producer building refers only to the kafka_producers section of this snapshot.
    • growthbook.rs (+132/−32) gained a new function, kafka_producers_from_config(Option<&Value>). As a result, the way producer config is obtained changed from "calling a DynamicConfig method" to "parsing the JSON Value directly".
    • In rules.rs (+65/−51), the lifetime was fine-tuned from rules_cache.resolve(entity_type, rules_override.as_deref()) to as_ref() (no change in meaning).

Why it matters Deciding which actions to block and what notice text to show for a restriction reason has moved into VF. Four layers of facts overlap in this commit.

  1. The authority over the "restriction reason" has been pulled into VF. Until now, the only information VF put on the engagement axis of the Verdict was a single reason enum, and which actions that reason blocks and what the UI text says were the job of downstream (home-mixer, the client). From this commit, the following three things happen inside VF:

    • LimitedActionsPolicies holds the reason → action list mapping, based on FeatureSwitches.
    • LimitedActionsCopy holds (action, user locale, country) → localized Prompt.
    • treatment.rs::thrift_action combines the two and puts the per-action Prompt list directly inside the Thrift payload LimitedEngagements.

    As a result, the client does not need to maintain its own localization or action mapping tables, and every decision of reason → action → text is declared as server-side data. This change pairs with Gen4+32, where treatment.rs was isolated as the rules → thrift/proto/metric projection layer. The previous commit "gathered the wire projection point into one file," and this commit "raised the richness of the data passing through that projection point up to the UI level."

  2. As LimitedEngagement changed to a structure that accumulates multiple reasons, the situation where the same tweet has several restriction reasons at once is now modeled explicitly.

    • Previously, once rules decided the engagement, any later ActionSpec::LimitedEngagement match was dropped (a dead-code path).
    • From this commit, if rules decide the engagement several times, the reasons accumulate in the Vec through limit.value.add(*reason).
    • In other words, when BlockedViewer (19 actions restricted) and ReadonlyViewer (14 actions restricted) overlap, VF merges the action lists of the two reasons into a union by action type and puts it in the payload together with the first reason verified — treatment.rs:128-148 and the test a_limit_sends_its_first_reason_and_the_union_of_its_reasons_policies.
    • Combinations such as ConversationControl with StaleTweet, or RootAuthorBlockedViewer with BlockedViewer, also become possible.

  3. abuse-enforcement-service switched to a "boot-time config snapshot" model.

    • After 2026-09-23, when the Rust rewrite was released, this service had been using live config through a DynamicConfig object. From this commit, the Kafka producer binding depends only on the boot-time JSON Value snapshot.
    • Instead, when the Kafka config in operation (topics, SASL authentication, bootstrap servers) changes, the restart_on_config_change module restarts the service to apply the new config.
    • This pattern keeps the kafka producer lifecycle simple (no hot swap).
    • It also makes config changes clearly visible in logs and metrics (restart events).
    • Finally, it provides a fail-safe that does not restart if config validation (validate(live)) fails.
    • This approach applies only to changes in core config (topic labels, kafka producer config), and everything else is still a live reload path.

  4. phoenix-rankall exposed an experiment group identifier and a QPS cap. Splitting out the CLI config module looks like an organizational refactor, but two figures are exposed in the process.

    • The first is SCORE_PREFIX = "phoenix.HomeExperiment1Lap7.". It means that the Phoenix ranking service publishes the scores of a specific experiment group on the Home surface, "Experiment1 Lap7", to a Kafka topic, and phoenix-rankall consumes only the scores with that specific prefix.
    • The name "Lap7" appears to be an experiment iteration number inferred.
    • The second is SID_BACKFILL_QPS_CAP = 800_000. It is the figure saying that the SID backfill QPS cap is 800k.
    • SID backfill is designed not to apply to all posts but to process only posts in a specific window (at most 1 hour, at least 3 minutes). SID_BACKFILL_MAX_AGE_HOURS = 1 appeared in an earlier commit and is kept this time as well.
    • Considering that the posts shown per day number in the hundreds of millions to billions, and reading this QPS cap together with the window design, the picture is that the SID embedding table is kept only for the subset of posts within the last 1 hour of freshness that the 800k QPS range covers.
    • Posts outside the freshness window remain without an SID or in a stale state.

2Generative ads retrieval GEAR deployed in Douyin Ads production — radar

Source arXiv 2609.39327 — arXiv, 2026-09-30 v1, paper. The title is "Generative End-to-end Ad Retrieval at Douyin". All 11 authors are affiliated with Douyin (ByteDance), which we confirmed in the HTML version.

Background Generative retrieval represents items as sequences of semantic tokens (Semantic ID) rather than ID embeddings, and replaces search with autoregressive generation. At industrial scale, two bottlenecks are known. Representation collapse is the problem where the item tokenizer converges to degenerate results under continual distribution shift, which makes end-to-end adaptation unstable. Item collision is the problem where, as the candidate pool grows huge, different items share the same token sequence and retrieval precision drops. The two problems are coupled: enlarging the codebook capacity to relieve collisions aggravates collapse. The paper's GEAR is a generative ads retrieval that addresses both bottlenecks, and the paper states that it is serving in Douyin Ads.

What we learnedA. GEAR jointly optimizes the tokenizer, generator, and reranker end to end

GEAR is a framework that jointly optimizes the tokenizer, generator, and reranker end to end. It has two core mechanisms:

  • BasisVQ reparameterizes the codebook as an orthogonal basis. This enables global gradient sharing and rigid spatial rotation of latent space, and stabilizes gradient dynamics without ad-hoc heuristics.
    • It extends BasisVQ to a prefix-aware BasisRQ, which greatly raises the expressiveness of the codebook within the same asymptotic time complexity.
  • The context-conditioned reranking head sits inside the generation process and disambiguates colliding items with minimal computational overhead.

"It currently serves hundreds of millions of daily active users on Douyin Ads, yielding substantial empirical improvements in extensive online A/B tests." — arXiv 2609.39327, 2026-09-30

Canon update Added a 2–4 line GEAR pointer to "new this month" in radar.md. Douyin (ByteDance) is outside the tracked services, so we do not fold it into a service canon.

Why it matters GEAR is the first explicit statement of SID-generation retrieval settled onto the ad surface of Douyin Ads. The SID-family predecessors Kuaishou HD-Rec/PushDualGen, Spotify GLIDE, and Tencent BARGE have already deployed SID-generation retrieval across various modalities and surfaces. Alibaba PailitaoGR reported only offline experiments with data built from Pailitao online search logs, and has no deployment or A/B statement verified — arXiv 2608.26658. It is the fourth statement in the past month under the angle that "generative retrieval is settling in at industrial scale", and the trend so far is as follows:

  • (a) Snap GRP v0.1 (2026-09-29) unified retrieval, ranking, and reward in a single encoder-decoder, and achieved serving latency −69% and A/B view time +0.82%.
  • (b) Tencent PCG TGR (2026-09-01) bundled ranking, retrieval, slate generation, and reason tokens into one frame and deployed it on 5 surfaces.
  • (c) Kuaishou OneTrans-V2 (2026-09-23) unified a 3-stage cascade into a single Transformer, and achieved GMV +9.74%.
  • (d) Today's GEAR explicitly addresses two structural bottlenecks of generative retrieval itself (representation collapse, item collision), and is serving hundreds of millions of DAU on Douyin Ads.

Unlike (a), (b), and (c), GEAR focuses only on retrieval, and targets the two known problems of that role with BasisVQ (codebook orthogonal basis reparameterization) and a context-conditioned reranking head. An earlier work on generative retrieval in the ads domain is Taobao TransRetrieval (2026-08-28). However, TransRetrieval is on the Transformer scaling side, treating the scaling problem as field heterogeneity, not on the SID-generation side. GEAR opens a Douyin line of SID-generation retrieval in ads, so whether ads retrieval converges toward generative approaches is the next checkpoint to watch.

  • GEAR (Douyin / ByteDance) deployed generative ad retrieval in Douyin Ads production for hundreds of millions of DAU. The affiliation, deployment scale, and mechanism are confirmed facts verified — arXiv 2609.39327.
    • It resolves the two bottlenecks end to end: representation collapse with BasisVQ (orthogonal basis codebook), and item collision with a context-conditioned reranking head.
