1Deployed a CVR ranker combining diffusion and LLM intent distillation to production — Xiaohongshu

Source GateDiffInt: Gate-Mediated Controllable Diffusion and Multi-Intent LLM Distillation for User Behavior Modeling — arXiv 2608.18764, v1 submitted 2026-08-19, v2 revised 2026-08-20, paper. The 10 authors are Jialong Duan, Zichen Zhang, Zirui Tu, Zheng Zhang, Zepeng Li, Qingyao Cui, Qinwen Wang, Yudan Liu, Luo Yang, and Yao Hu. The abstract page lists no affiliation; we confirmed that the author email domain in the PDF metadata is xiaohongshu.com.

Background When an industry ranker predicts CVR (conversion rate, the probability that a click or view leads to an actual conversion), the standard approach is to feed the user's recent behavior sequence into an encoder and extract a user state vector. A behavior sequence is a series of events such as visits, clicks, likes, saves, and purchases. This sequence has two fundamental problems. The first is noise: accidental clicks, mistakes, and session drop-offs dilute the true intent. The second is that intent is not explicit. The sequence itself does not label "what the user wants," so the model has to extract it implicitly.

So far, approaches in this field have mostly followed the SIM/DIN/BST lineage, which treats intent as a latent factor in attention pooling. This paper (GateDiffInt) adds a diffusion generative model and an LLM teacher to address each of the two problems explicitly.

What we learnedA. Two mechanisms are added to the layer that strengthens the ranker's input representation

  • Instead of changing the ranker's whole backbone structure, GateDiffInt adds two new mechanisms to the layer that strengthens the input representation verified.
    • "gate-mediated controllable diffusion" is a denoising module placed in front of the sequence encoder.
    • "multi-intent LLM distillation" is a four-vector intent stream attached alongside the encoder.
    • The representation that fuses the two streams with attention goes into the CVR prediction head.

What we learnedB. Diffusion with gate-controlled noise reconstructs the behavior sequence

Diffusion is a model family that originated in image generation and is known for a training scheme that corrupts an image into noise and then reverses the process. Applied to sequences from a denoising perspective, it can train a model that takes the original as the target and reconstructs it from a noisy input.

GateDiffInt describes this process as a "controllable forward diffusion process with dual gating". It means that gates control how much noise is added and at which event positions. The gates are also trained. The denoising output flows into the CVR head, and the final loss is backpropagated.

What we learnedC. An LLM teacher extracts four kinds of intent and distills them into a lightweight student

LLM intent distillation (training in which a student model learns to follow the output of a teacher model) is a separate axis from diffusion. The LLM teacher looks at the user sequence and extracts structured intent in four kinds. The teacher's model size is not disclosed.

  • long-term: long-term taste
  • short-term: focus of the recent session
  • latent: implicitly observed interest
  • conversion: intent leading to a purchase

The lightweight student receives these four vectors by distillation, and the representation fused with attention is used in the CVR prediction head. LLM inference is used only in offline training and only the student remains in the serving path, so serving latency stays at a level similar to existing rankers.

What we learnedD. The authors are from Xiaohongshu, and the abstract states primary traffic deployment

  • The author affiliation is Xiaohongshu verified. The contact email domain in the PDF metadata is xiaohongshu.com, and we confirmed it as a Xiaohongshu production ranker paper.
  • The abstract states primary traffic deployment and serving at the scale of hundreds of millions of daily active users verified.
    • The abstract wrote: "deployed to primary traffic, confirming both effectiveness and production readiness", "serving hundreds of millions of daily active users".
    • However, specific uplift figures (e.g., CVR/GMV/CTR) are not in the abstract. They are likely to be released in v3 or in the full paper after acceptance at CIKM/KDD inferred.

"we propose GateDiffInt, a novel framework that couples a controllable forward diffusion process with dual gating to enhance and denoise behavior sequences ... A large language model then acts as teacher to distill four structured intents — long-term, short-term, latent, and conversion — into a lightweight student model." — GateDiffInt abstract, 2026-08-20 v2

"GateDiffInt delivers substantial GMV improvements ... deployed to primary traffic, confirming both effectiveness and production readiness." — Same abstract

Why it matters This paper carries two overlapping signals. One is that Xiaohongshu is publishing production papers on several layers of its ranking stack at the same time; the other is that one more approach that keeps the LLM out of serving has accumulated.

  1. Xiaohongshu published two production papers in a short period.
    • While OneModel heads toward unifying the rankers of three surfaces (organic, ads, and store), GateDiffInt is a branch where the same company separately experimented with a sequence encoder specialized for the CVR subtask.
    • This is a company-level pattern of resource investment similar to what we confirmed at Kuaishou (production papers PushDualGen and DrEM, benchmark paper HD-Rec).
  2. One more approach that keeps the LLM out of serving has accumulated. In the LLM-native ranking thread, two branches, hot-path LLM and offline LLM, are taking shape.
    • GateDiffInt uses LLM inference only as an offline teacher, and only the student (diffusion and attention) remains in the serving path.
    • It is similar to MetaStrategy's "LLM as a nearline policy compiler that is not in serving," but the direction differs. Nearline is an approach that precomputes close to real-time, separated from request handling.
    • In MetaStrategy, the LLM produces policy parameters and a deterministic compiler builds the later stages. In GateDiffInt, the LLM produces intent representations and the student receives them by distillation and serves them.
    • Netflix GenRec and Meta ConnectionMind are two cases of "the LLM is in the serving hot path." MetaStrategy and GateDiffInt are two cases of "the LLM stays outside serving as a teacher or compiler."
  3. Separately, yesterday's daily arXiv triage (the task of skimming titles and abstracts of new cs.IR submissions and selecting only those that pass the acceptance criteria) judged this paper incorrectly.
    • Yesterday's daily recorded: "triaged 30 new arXiv cs.IR submissions from 8/19–8/20, and 0 items passed." In fact, this paper satisfies all the pass criteria (platform-company authors, production deployment or A/B claim, mechanism novelty, scale).
    • It was missed because the quick skim of the abstract did not catch the phrases "hundreds of millions of DAU" and "primary traffic". In future triage, these two phrases should be added as explicit filter conditions.
